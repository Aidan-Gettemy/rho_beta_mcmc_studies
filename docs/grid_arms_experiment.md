# Grid-refinement study of global pCN: three observation arms (2026-11-04)

Code: `brho/gridstudy.py` (setup, arms, data, likelihoods, chain), `brho/kle.py` (`build_kle`, method `nystrom`, `basis="canonical"`), `experiments/run_grid_chains.py`,
`run_grid_post.py`, `run_grid_compare.py`, `grid_fisher_check.py`; tests `tests/test_gridstudy.py`;
jobs `cluster/slurm/grid_*.sbatch` (nine, generated by `cluster/gen_grid_sbatch.py`) and
`cluster/submit_grid_arms.sh`.

## 1. Question

How does global pCN behave when the same Darcy slab problem is solved on a refined grid (16x16, 32x32,
64x64 on the same unit square)? Because the parameter is the same 20-dimensional KLE vector on every grid, the
posterior lives in R^20 for all of them. The mesh changes (a) the forward model slightly and (b) how much data
can be recorded. Three observation arms separate these effects:

| arm | observations | noise and likelihood | purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 `check` | checkerboard cells of the grid: 128 / 512 / 2048 | fixed *normalised* noise, relative-error likelihood (as in the MSM study) | data grows with the grid; the group's likelihood |
| 2 `fixed` | 128 on every grid, in the grid cell closest to the centre of each 16x16 checkerboard cell | as arm 1 | mesh effect with the data held fixed |
| 3 `abs` | checkerboard cells of the grid: 128 / 512 / 2048 | fixed *per-observation* noise sigma_abs = 0.0236, Gaussian likelihood | data grows and so does the information |

At 16x16 arms 1 and 2 are the same experiment (identical data and likelihood; a built-in check). Arm 3 at 16x16
has the same likelihood *weight* as arm 1 (Section 5) but its data carry about 11 times more noise (3.4% against 0.3%
per observation, see below), so its 16x16 posterior is not expected to equal arm 1's; arm 3 is compared with itself
across grids.

## 2. Problem

Dirichlet permeameter (pressure 1 left, 0 right, no flow top and bottom), cell-centred finite volume with
harmonic transmissibilities (`brho/fv.py`, `physics2d.Darcy2D`), squared-exponential kernel with correlation
length 0.2, unit variance, log k = T theta, theta ~ N(0, I_20).

**KLE.** `build_kle(..., method="nystrom", nq=16, basis="canonical", r_max=20)`: the full 2D weighted Nystrom
problem (no separability used) is solved once on a 16 x 16-node tensor Gauss-Legendre rule on the unit square,
independent of the simulation grid, and the 20 eigenfunctions are extended to the cell centres of each grid by the
Nystrom formula (the construction of the MATLAB code). T_16, T_32, T_64 are therefore samples of the same 20
eigenfunctions (eigenvalues identical; tested), and one theta is the same continuous field on all grids (block-averaged
fields differ by 0.5-0.6% in log k and 0.04-0.05% in pressure, which is point versus cell-average sampling). 16 nodes
per axis reproduce the 40-node eigenvalues to 5e-11 and the fields to 2e-10 (8 nodes: 2e-2 and 2e-3).
The first 20 modes hold 97.1% of the variance; the 20th and 21st eigenvalues (0.00460, 0.00375) are not tied
(the 21st/22nd are), so the truncation splits no degenerate group (`build_kle` never splits a group and
`GridKle` refuses a cut that would).

**Reproducibility of the basis.** On a square the isotropic kernel has exactly degenerate pairs (the modes (i, j)
and (j, i)), and an eigensolver returns eigenvectors only up to sign and, inside a degenerate eigenspace, up to
rotation; which one depends on the LAPACK build and library version. With eigh's basis the same theta* was a
*different field* on two machines (sigma_abs 0.0167 with the sandbox's basis, 0.0240 with the cluster's, found by
`test_sigma_abs_is_eps_times_16_data_norm`). `basis="canonical"` fixes the basis of every degenerate group
through the eigenspace projector (fixed smooth reference functions are projected onto the eigenspace and
Gram-Schmidt orthonormalised), so T depends only on the kernel and the quadrature rule. It leaves the covariance
T T' unchanged; tested by scrambling the eigensolver's output (random rotations inside tied groups).

**Truth.** theta* = `default_rng(2026).standard_normal(20)`, the first 20 entries of the MSM study's truth.

**Forward model across grids.** For the same theta* the block-averaged pressure differs between 32 and 16 by 0.04%
and between 64 and 16 by 0.05% (relative L2), against 0.3% noise, so the data generated on different grids are
statistically consistent and the grid effect is small compared with the noise. (Measured once at theta*;
`experiments/plot_grid_kle.py` also draws the first five eigenfunctions and the reference field on each grid:
`tables/grid_arms/kle_eigenfunctions.png`, `kle_reference_fields.png`.)

## 3. Observation sets

* `check`, `abs`: cells with (i + j) even, `Darcy2D.obs_mask`: 128, 512, 2048.
* `fixed`: for each of the 128 cells (I, J) of the 16x16 checkerboard, the grid cell closest to its centre
  (`centre_cell_indices`). On 16x16 this is the cell itself. On 32x32 and 64x64 the centre of a 16-cell is a
  vertex shared by four equally close fine cells; **the tie is broken by the lower-left of the central four**, so
  the recorded location is at most half a fine-cell diagonal from the centre (0.022 on 32, 0.011 on 64; tested).
  Averaging the four central values would give the exact centre value but is not a grid cell; it was not used.

## 4. Noise and likelihoods

Let n be the number of observations, p the noiseless observed pressures at theta*, xi ~ N(0, I_n) the next n
standard normals of the seed-2026 stream after theta* (arms with the same observation set share xi).

* Arms 1, 2: d = p + sigma_p xi with sigma_p = 0.003 ||p|| / sqrt(n) (2.09e-3 at theta*); likelihood
  Phi(theta) = ||d - O p(theta)||^2 / (2 * 0.003^2 ||d||^2). The expected Phi at the truth is about 0.5 whatever
  n is (a note already made in the MSM study: the global-norm likelihood is tempered by n).
* Arm 3: d = p + sigma_abs xi, Phi(theta) = ||d - O p(theta)||^2 / (2 sigma_abs^2), the proper Gaussian
  log-likelihood; the expected Phi at the truth is n / 2.
* Diagnostic in all arms: relative error ||d - O p(theta)|| / ||d||.

## 5. Why sigma_abs = 0.0236

**Observation that motivated arm 3.** With the relative-error likelihood the information per observation does not
grow with n: the numerator and ||d||^2 both scale with n. At theta* the Fisher-information proxy
F = w J'J (J the Jacobian of the observed pressures with respect to theta, w the likelihood weight;
`experiments/grid_fisher_check.py`, `tables/grid_arms/fisher_proxy.md`) is

| arm | grid | n obs | trace F | relative to 16x16 | data-dominated directions (eig > 1) | max eig |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 `check` | 16 / 32 / 64 | 128 / 512 / 2048 | 2845 / 2877 / 2885 | 1.00 / 1.01 / 1.01 | 15 / 15 / 15 | 1.5e3 |
| 2 `fixed` | 16 / 32 / 64 | 128 | 2845 / 2809 / 2851 | 1.00 / 0.99 / 1.00 | 15 / 15 / 15 | 1.5e3 |
| 3 `abs` | 16 / 32 / 64 | 128 / 512 / 2048 | 2845 / 11510 / 46190 | 1.00 / 4.05 / 16.24 | 15 / 15 / 18 | 1.5e3 / 6.2e3 / 2.5e4 |

So arms 1 and 2 are both expected to give nearly the same posterior on every grid. A "more data" arm needs a
likelihood whose information grows with the data, i.e. a fixed per-observation noise.

**The choice.** sigma_abs = 0.003 ||p_obs|| evaluated on the noiseless 16x16 checkerboard data at theta*
(`sigma_abs_reference()`; ||p_obs|| = 7.8743, sigma_abs = 0.023623, i.e. 3.4% of the rms pressure 0.6960 of those
data). Reasons:

1. **Anchored to the existing arms.** At 16x16 the likelihood weight is w = 1/sigma_abs^2 = 1792, against
   1791 for arm 1 (w = 1/(0.003^2 ||d||^2)): the same likelihood function of theta. The *data* are not the same: in
   arms 1 and 2 the noise is generated at 0.3% of the rms pressure (sigma_p = 2.09e-3) while the likelihood
   assumes sigma = 0.003 ||d|| (11 times larger, because the global-norm likelihood is tempered by n); in arm 3 the
   data noise equals the likelihood's (0.0236). So at 16x16 arm 3 has the same sensitivity to theta as arms 1 and 2
   but noisier data, and its posterior is centred about one posterior sd away from theirs (the posterior of a
   consistent model rather than that of nearly noiseless data). From 16 to 64 the only thing that changes in arm 3 is
   the number of observations at fixed per-observation noise.
2. **A consistent noise model.** Data are generated with sigma_abs and the likelihood uses sigma_abs, so Phi at
   the truth is about n/2 (realised 78.7 / 289.8 / 1031.7 against 64 / 256 / 1024), unlike arms 1 and 2, whose
   likelihood is deliberately tempered (expected Phi at the truth about 0.5).
3. **Information that grows with the data by the intended factor**: 1x, 4.05x, 16.24x in the trace of F (the
   number of directions with information above the prior's rises from 15 to 15 to 18 of 20, and the number above 100
   from 4 to 8 to 11).
4. **Feasible for pCN.** The adapted step size was expected to shrink roughly with the square root of the largest
   information eigenvalue (1.5e3 to 2.5e4, a factor of about 4 on the finest grid). The pilot (Section 9) agrees:
   the adapted step falls from about 0.033 (16x16) to 0.008 (64x64) with acceptance near 25%, far from the floor
   1e-4, so 100,000 iterations are a reasonable budget; whether it suffices for MPSRF < 1.2 is for the real runs to show.

**Alternative considered and not used as the default:** sigma_abs = 0.003 * rms(p) = 0.0021 (0.3% per
observation), the literal 0.3% noise level. It is 128 times sharper than arms 1 and 2 at 16x16 (the likelihood
weight is n = 128 times larger), and 2048 times sharper at 64x64, so the pCN step size would have to shrink by
more than an order of magnitude; non-convergence within 100,000 iterations is likely and the 16x16 case would
no longer match the other arms. It can be added later as arm 3b by passing `--sigma-abs 0.0021`.

## 6. What the study is expected to show (hypotheses, not results)

* Arms 1 and 2: mesh-independent posteriors in theta (step size, acceptance, iterations to MPSRF < 1.2 and
  posterior sd within sampling error of the 16x16 values).
* Arm 3: the posterior concentrates with refinement (mean posterior sd of theta falls, more directions are
  data-dominated) and pCN slows down accordingly (smaller adapted step size, more iterations to MPSRF < 1.2,
  possibly not within 100,000 iterations at 64x64).
* A difference between arms 1 and 2 at 32 or 64 would point at the observation locations or the
  discretisation, not at the amount of data.

Observed outcomes are added in Section 9 and in the report when the cluster runs are back.

## 7. Settings

64 chains per configuration, N = 100,000 iterations, step size adapted to 25% acceptance for the first
M = 10,000 iterations then frozen, s0 = 0.2, lower bound 1e-4 on the step (the MSM runs used 1e-3; arms 1 and 2
adapt to 0.03 or so, but arm 3 on a fine grid may need smaller), chain seeds SeedSequence([7, chain]), theta
saved every 50 iterations, relative error / acceptance / Phi every iteration. The posterior fields use 10,000
pooled post-M samples. Diagnostics follow the group's MPSRF convention (window from iteration 1,000, adaptation
included, growing to the end, skip 2, curve every 15 saved samples; text written to `post_summary.json`);
MPSRF is computed on the 20 theta coordinates.

## 8. How to run

```
bash cluster/submit_grid_arms.sh              # DRY=1 to print the nine sbatch commands
# after the jobs finish (needs no chains):
BRHO_ROOT=runs/grid_arms_2026-11 python experiments/run_grid_compare.py
# commit, from the cluster checkout: runs/grid_arms_2026-11/*/summary.json and */post/ (chains.npz is git-ignored)
```

Each job runs its tests, then the chains, then the post-processing. Timing in a 2-core sandbox:
0.4 / 1.7 / 5 ms per forward solve at 16 / 32 / 64 (about 40 s, 3 min, 9 min per 100,000-iteration chain);
the cluster is expected to be faster.

## 9. Pilot (sandbox, 8 chains, 20,000 iterations, M = 4,000; step-size/timing check only)

| run | n obs | adapted step s | post-M acceptance | median relerr (at truth) | Phi at truth (expected) | s per chain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| check 16 | 128 | 0.037 | 0.268 | 0.0118 (0.0033) | 7.7 (0.6) | 9 |
| abs 16 | 128 | 0.033 | 0.278 | 0.0362 (0.0375) | 73.4 (78.7) | 10 |
| abs 64 | 2048 | 0.0084 (max 0.0105) | 0.278 | 0.0340 (0.0340) | 1031.7 (1024) | 93 |

* The arm-3 step shrinks by a factor of about 4 from 16x16 to 64x64, as the Fisher proxy predicts; no chain is near the
  step floor. A 100,000-iteration chain on 64x64 takes about 8 minutes in the 2-core sandbox.
* The pilots are too short to say anything about MPSRF (about 2 after 20,000 iterations at 16x16); the 32x32
  pilots and the arm 1/2 runs at 64x64 were not needed for the design and are left to the cluster runs.
* The 16x16 pilot posteriors of arms 1 and 3 differ (Gaussian-approximation KL about 10): arm 3 has noisier data
  (Section 5, item 1), as expected.

## 10. Caveats

* One data set and one seed set; differences of tens of percent in iterations to MPSRF < 1.2 between single
  runs are not necessarily significant.
* The 20-mode truncation leaves 2.9% of the prior variance out; truth and inference use the same 20 modes.
* Data differ slightly between grids because the forward model is discretised differently (about 0.1% of the
  pressure, below the 0.3% noise) and, in arms 1 and 3, because the observation sets differ.
* Arm 3 may not reach MPSRF < 1.2 within 100,000 iterations on the finest grid; if so that is a result, and
  longer chains for that run are the follow-up.
